Roman Carbonara

Serves 4 · 400 g pasta · Roman method: Monosilio, Roscioli, L’Arcangelo

Ingredients

  • 400 g pasta
  • 250 g guanciale, 1 cm cubes
  • 6 egg yolks
  • 80 g Pecorino Romano, grated (about ¾ cup), plus extra
  • Black pepper, plenty

Method

1

Render the guanciale slowly until crisp. Save the fat.

2

Whisk the yolks, pecorino, pepper and 1 tbsp of the fat into a paste.

3

Cook the pasta 2 minutes short of the package time. Save 2 cups of the water.

4

Hold the egg bowl over the pasta pot and whisk until thick like custard, 1 to 2 minutes.

5

Add the pasta and ½ cup pasta water to the egg bowl. Toss over the steam until glossy, 1 to 2 minutes.

6

Off the heat, stir in the guanciale. Finish with pecorino and pepper.

If it tightens or looks grainy: off the steam, add a splash of pasta water.

Cacio e Pepe

No quantities in the original · From Quora

Ingredients

  • Pasta
  • Pecorino Romano, the real thing, grated
  • Black pepper, freshly ground
  • Starchy pasta water

Method

1

Cook the pasta and pull it 1 minute before the package time. Don’t drain it.

2

In a large pan, warm a couple of spoons of pasta water. Grind in pepper and toast it gently.

3

In a bowl, whisk a little pecorino with a spoon of pasta water. Keep adding cheese and splashes of water until it’s a smooth slurry.

4

Move the pasta into the pepper pan with its water. Add the cheese slurry and stir until silky.

5

Serve right away.

Don’t overcook the pasta, be patient whisking, and keep the heat low or it turns lumpy.

Colatura Spaghetti

Serves 4–6 · under 15 min · Raffaella in Cetara, via Gustiamo

Ingredients

  • 500 g spaghetti
  • Colatura: 1 tbsp per 100 g pasta (about ⅔ bottle)
  • Extra virgin olive oil: 2 tbsp per 100 g pasta
  • ½ garlic clove, minced
  • Parsley, a medium bunch, minced
  • Chili peppers, to taste

Your note for pici (150 g)

  • 2½ tbsp colatura
  • 3 tbsp olive oil

Method

1

Boil the pasta in UNSALTED water until al dente.

2

Drain and save the pasta water.

3

Put the pasta in a serving bowl and add everything else, raw.

4

Mix well. If it’s dry, add pasta water a spoonful at a time.

5

Serve right away.

Never add cheese. Toasted breadcrumbs or lemon zest are fine. Colatura stays out of the fridge.

Spaghetti alla Nerano

Serves 4 · 45 min · Gustiamo, with colatura

Ingredients

  • 400 g spaghetti
  • Coarse sea salt
  • 1 kg zucchini, sliced ¼ inch (mandoline)
  • 4 tbsp extra virgin olive oil
  • 2 garlic cloves, peeled
  • 1 tbsp colatura, or more
  • 2 cups grated Vacche Rosse or parmigiano (50–60 g)
  • 20–30 basil leaves

Pasta · Method

Spaghetti alla Nerano

Serves 4 · 45 min · Gustiamo, with colatura

Method

1

Heat 2 tbsp oil with 1 garlic clove. Discard the garlic when it browns.

2

Cook half the zucchini over medium until soft and browned in spots, about 15 min. Set aside in a tall jug.

3

Add 2 more tbsp oil and the second clove (discard when brown). Cook the rest of the zucchini until soft and browned.

4

Start the spaghetti in salted water.

5

Blend the set-aside zucchini with 1 cup pasta water and the colatura. Stir it into the pan.

6

2 minutes before al dente, add the spaghetti, most of the basil, ⅔ of the cheese and 1 cup pasta water. Finish cooking in the sauce.

7

Serve with the rest of the basil and cheese.

Spaghetti alla Carrettiera

Serves 4 · 400 g spaghetti · Gustiamo and @leftburner

Ingredients

  • 1 sachet dried porcini (50 g)
  • Extra virgin olive oil
  • 1 shallot, finely minced
  • 4 garlic cloves, peeled, whole
  • 1 jar Corbarino tomatoes (680 g)
  • Chili peppers, to taste
  • 400 g spaghetti
  • 1 jar bluefin tuna (320 g), drained, oil saved
  • 3–5 caperberries, rinsed, soaked, minced
  • Parsley, minced
  • Salt and pepper

Pasta · Method

Spaghetti alla Carrettiera

Serves 4 · 400 g spaghetti · Gustiamo and @leftburner

Method

1

Soak the porcini in warm water 30 min. Lift out, strain the broth and keep ¾ cup. Chop the mushrooms.

2

1 tbsp oil, medium-low: cook the shallot until soft, 3 min. Add mushrooms and a pinch of salt, 5–7 min. Set aside.

3

A little more oil, then the garlic until golden, about 3 min.

4

Add tomatoes, the ¾ cup broth, chili, caperberries, salt and pepper. Simmer 10+ min until thick.

5

Stir in the mushrooms and tuna (keep some big chunks). Lower the heat.

6

Cook the spaghetti in well-salted water, about 8 min. Save ¼ cup water.

7

Take out the garlic. Toss pasta, sauce, the ¼ cup water and about 5 tbsp tuna oil over very low heat.

8

Finish with parsley and more tuna oil.

Busiate with Pesto alla Trapanese

Serves 5 · 15 min · Gustiamo

Ingredients

  • 500 g busiate
  • 1 cup Piennolo tomatoes (250 g, ½ jar)
  • 60 g fresh basil
  • 100 g unpeeled Noto almonds
  • 1 garlic clove
  • ½ cup extra virgin olive oil (120 ml)
  • 1 tsp sea salt
  • ½ tsp chili peppers, or to taste
  • ½ cup grated ricotta salata or pecorino (50 g)

Method

1

Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil.

2

Grind the tomatoes, basil, ¾ of the almonds, garlic, oil, salt and chili to a medium-fine grind. Add the last almonds at the end for bigger pieces.

3

Cook the busiate al dente. Save ⅓ cup pasta water.

4

In a serving bowl, toss busiate, pesto and the water until coated.

5

Serve and grate cheese over each plate.

You had two Gustiamo copies of this. They’re the same dish; the older one used Corbarino tomatoes, 100 ml oil and made the cheese optional.

Pasta e Ceci alla Romana

Serves 4 · Gustiamo

Ingredients

  • 300 g dried chickpeas
  • 150 g mezzi paccheri
  • 1 tbsp tomato concentrate
  • 4 spoons extra virgin olive oil
  • 3 anchovies, rinsed and filleted
  • 1 garlic clove, finely sliced
  • 1 sprig rosemary
  • Sea salt and pepper

Pasta · Method

Pasta e Ceci alla Romana

Serves 4 · Gustiamo

Method

1

Soak the chickpeas about 8 hours.

2

Drain. Simmer in unsalted water with the rosemary about 2 hours, until soft. Then salt them.

3

Drain, saving half the cooking water.

4

Sauté the garlic in the oil. Add the anchovies and let them melt.

5

Add the tomato concentrate and about 8 spoons of chickpea water. Simmer 5 min until smooth, then add the chickpeas (mash some for creaminess).

6

Cook the pasta in salted water. Drain al dente, save some water, and add the pasta to the pan.

7

Loosen with pasta water until creamy. Serve with black pepper and oil.

Steps 1 to 5 can be done the day before.

Grandma Sasso’s Ceci Pasta

Written down by Mom · “I watched Grandma Sasso in her kitchen… writing down how she put it all together.” — M

Ingredients

  • Dried chickpeas
  • ½ cup olive oil
  • 2 garlic cloves, mashed
  • 2 wooden spoons of tomato paste
  • About 4½ cups water (3 of the green glasses)
  • 2 tsp salt
  • Basil, 5 or 6 shakes
  • Regular spaghetti, broken up
  • Pepper at the table

Method

1

The night before: soak the beans, water to the top of the pot.

2

Don’t drain. Add water to the top and simmer until cooked through, with about half the water left, still covering the beans.

3

Sauce: sauté the garlic in the oil quickly, don’t let it burn. Take it out.

4

Add the tomato paste; mash and mix it into the oil.

5

Add the water, then the beans with their water, the salt and the basil.

6

Cook the broken spaghetti, drain, and combine. Pepper at the table.

Buon appetito. Xoxo M

Spinach and Ricotta Pasta

Serves 4 · From Quora

Ingredients

  • 320 g pasta
  • About 500 g fresh spinach
  • 200 g ricotta
  • 1 garlic clove, whole, skin on
  • 2 tbsp extra virgin olive oil
  • Salt
  • A little chili (optional)
  • A handful of grated Parmigiano Reggiano

Method

1

Boil salted water and start the pasta. Stir the ricotta and Parmigiano smooth. Roughly chop the washed spinach.

2

4 minutes before the pasta is done, heat the oil in a big pan with the garlic and chili.

3

2 minutes before al dente, add the spinach to the pasta pot.

4

When the spinach wilts, take out the garlic. Drain pasta and spinach into the pan and toss 1 minute.

5

Stir in the ricotta and serve right away.

Mackerel Pasta Fredda

Serves 3 · 15 min + chilling · Gustiamo

Ingredients

  • 250 g caserecci (½ bag)
  • Coarse sea salt
  • 1 jar mackerel in oil (290 g)
  • ½ jar Piennolo tomatoes (260 g)
  • ½ jar black olives (95 g), optional
  • 2 tbsp salted capers, rinsed, optional
  • 1 tbsp parsley, chopped

Method

1

Cook the caserecci very al dente, about 6 min. It keeps softening as it cools.

2

Pour the mackerel oil into a cup. Lift out the fish.

3

In a big bowl, combine tomatoes, capers, olives and half the mackerel. Keep the big chunks for later.

4

Drain the pasta well, tip it onto the tomatoes, add the mackerel oil and mix hard. Fold in the rest of the fish.

5

Cover with a towel, let it reach room temperature, then refrigerate.

6

Serve cold or at room temperature with parsley and oil from the olives.

It’s even better the next day.

Fresh Egg Pasta

Serves 4–6 · The Kitchn

Ingredients

  • 2 cups flour, plus extra for rolling
  • ½ tsp salt
  • 3 large eggs

Equipment

  • Bowl and fork
  • Pasta machine (or a rolling pin)
  • Baking sheet and a clean dish towel

Pasta · Method

Fresh Egg Pasta

Serves 4–6 · The Kitchn

Method

1

Whisk flour and salt in a bowl. Make a deep well, crack in the eggs and whisk them.

2

Pull in flour from the sides bit by bit until it forms a very soft dough. You may not use all the flour.

3

Turn it out. Fold and knead, adding flour as needed, until smooth and elastic with few air bubbles when cut.

4

Rest covered at least 30 min. (Or fridge up to 24 hours; bring back to room temperature.)

5

Divide into 4 on a floured sheet. Keep the pieces floured and covered.

6

Roll each on the widest setting 2–3 times, fold in thirds, roll again. Then go thinner one setting at a time (6 or 7 on the KitchenAid for fettuccine).

7

Cut into 12-inch sheets, then into noodles. Toss with flour and gather into loose nests.

8

Cook in salted boiling water 4–5 min. Or air-dry until brittle, or freeze up to 3 months.

Food processor: pulse everything into a dough, then knead. By hand: roll thin with a pin, flour, roll up and slice.

Risotto, the Method

A technique, no quantities · From Quora

Ingredients

  • Arborio, Carnaroli or Vialone Nano rice
  • Butter or extra virgin olive oil
  • Onion or shallot, diced
  • Dry white wine, good enough to drink
  • Hot stock
  • Butter and grated cheese to finish

Risotto · Method

Risotto, the Method

A technique, no quantities · From Quora

Method

1

In a wide, tall pan, soften the onion in the fat. Add the rice and toast it, stirring, until translucent and it starts to “sing.”

2

Add the wine, lower the heat, stir once. Let it evaporate until the smell turns mellow.

3

Add a lot of stock at once, enough to cover the rice by about 1 cm. Stir once and leave it alone.

4

If you held some back, add it later, stirring only once each time.

5

When it’s al dente but still loose (“all’onda”), turn off the heat. Add butter and cheese and stir hard for a few minutes.

6

Rest 2–4 minutes, stir again and serve.

Don’t stir often or with a metal spoon: it breaks the grains and the rice sticks.

Fresh Tomato Risotto

Serves 4–5 · 35 min · Gustiamo, after David Tanis

Ingredients

  • Extra virgin olive oil
  • 1 medium onion, diced
  • 1 garlic clove, peeled, whole
  • Fine sea salt and black pepper
  • 1½ cups Vialone Nano rice (320 g)
  • ½ cup white wine
  • 360 g Piennolo tomatoes (⅔ jar), or thick-skinned cherry tomatoes
  • Chili peppers in oil, to taste
  • About 8 cups vegetable stock or parmesan-rind broth
  • ½ cup grated Vacche Rosse (20 g), plus more
  • Fresh basil, torn

Risotto · Method

Fresh Tomato Risotto

Serves 4–5 · 35 min · Gustiamo, after David Tanis

Method

1

A few tbsp oil, medium heat: cook onion, garlic, salt and pepper until soft, 8–10 min. Bring the stock to a low simmer.

2

Add the rice and stir to coat. Take out the garlic.

3

When the grains turn translucent, add the wine and stir as it evaporates.

4

Add chili and tomatoes; stir until dry. Then add hot stock a ladle at a time, letting each absorb.

5

Cook until al dente and creamy, 15–20 min.

6

Off the heat, stir in the cheese and a drizzle of oil.

7

Serve with more cheese, oil and basil.

Parmesan-rind broth as the stock takes the umami way up.

Marinara

One large can of tomatoes · Saved note

Ingredients

  • 1 large can San Marzano tomatoes
  • 1 large tbsp tomato paste
  • 2–3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 cup white wine
  • 2–3 bay leaves
  • ½ tsp marjoram
  • ¼ tsp crushed red pepper, or black pepper
  • ¼ cup olive oil
  • 1 large onion, minced
  • Salt

Method

1

Caramelize the onion in the oil until golden brown.

2

Add the garlic for 1 minute.

3

Add everything else at once: tomatoes, paste, bay, marjoram, wine, pepper.

4

Salt to taste. Cook on low 30–40 minutes.

Sunday gravy note: cook the tomato paste until it starts to caramelize before the tomatoes go in, and add fresh basil at the end.

Gravy from Almost Anything

About 2 cups · Saved note: “the secret is that there is no secret”

Ingredients

  • A skillet with browned bits in it
  • 2 cups water
  • 1 tbsp butter
  • 1 tbsp flour

Method

1

Boil the 2 cups of water in the unwashed skillet to lift the browned crust.

2

Strain it through a sieve and let the dark liquid cool.

3

Wash the skillet. Melt the butter, add the flour and cook just until it loses the raw-flour taste.

4

Slowly add the liquid, whisking, until thick.

Bone Broth

About a gallon · 10–24 hours · Saved note

Ingredients

  • 1 gallon water (16 cups)
  • 2 tbsp vinegar (1 oz)
  • 3–4 lb bones and tissue
  • Optional: vegetables, spices, herbs (sage and thyme are excellent)

Method

1

Put everything in a large pot. Bones can go in whole, sawn or broken into chunks.

2

Bring to a boil, then lower to a simmer.

3

Simmer at least a few hours, best 10 to 24. Top up water as it evaporates and stir now and then.

4

Or use a crock pot set to 160°F or a little higher.

5

Ladle it out by the cupful, or strain out the bones.

Sauces & Stocks · Tips

Stock: Tips and a Dash of Something

A reference card · A Michelin-trained cook on Quora, and your “good stock” note

Tips

1

Always start from cold water.

2

Reduce uncovered; no pressure cooker.

3

Don’t stir every few minutes.

4

Skim the foam and fat constantly.

5

Strain gently; don’t break the solids.

6

Fish stock (fumet) cooks much shorter.

7

Soak bones in ice water in the fridge 12 hours first.

8

Use first-quality ingredients.

Good stock, good stock, good stock.

Ham Bone for Soup

Storing it and starting the soup · Your “Jambo” note

Ingredients

  • A ham bone (jamón)
  • A cloth
  • A little oil, not olive oil

Method

1

Wrap it in a cloth. Rub the cut side with a little oil so it doesn’t dry out. Never aluminum foil.

2

For soup: start the bone in cold water.

3

When it boils (about 2 hours), skim off all the foam.

4

Take the piece out, rinse it, put it back, and make your soup.

Agrodolce Meatballs

About 80 meatballs · 1 hour · Gustiamo, from Fabrizia Lanza

Ingredients

  • 4 slices bread, crusts off
  • 1 cup whole milk (250 ml)
  • 500 g minced pork
  • 500 g minced veal
  • 1 medium red onion, minced
  • ¼ cup pine nuts (35 g)
  • ¼ cup grated Vacche Rosse (10 g)
  • 1 or 2 eggs
  • 1 tbsp + 1 tsp colatura
  • Parsley and mint, a small bunch each, chopped
  • Black pepper; salt, go easy
  • Extra virgin olive oil for frying
  • ¾ cup saba (180 ml)
  • ¾ cup white wine (180 ml)

Meat, Chicken & Fish · Method

Agrodolce Meatballs

About 80 meatballs · 1 hour · Gustiamo, from Fabrizia Lanza

Method

1

Soak the bread in the milk.

2

Mix pork, veal, onion, pine nuts, cheese, 1 egg, colatura and herbs. Season lightly; the colatura is salty.

3

Squeeze the bread and work it in by hand. Dry? Add the second egg.

4

Roll heaping-tablespoon balls, about 80.

5

Heat about 4 inches of oil to 325°F. Fry in batches until cooked, about 5 min. Drain on a rack.

6

To serve: simmer the saba and wine in a large pan, add the meatballs and cook about 5 min until the sauce reduces.

7

Cool slightly so the sauce thickens.

Diner Burgers

7 oz patties, 80/20 chuck · Saved note

Ingredients

  • 80/20 ground chuck, 6–8 oz per burger (7 is the sweet spot)
  • ¼ tsp salt and ⅛ tsp pepper per side
  • A slice of cheese each
  • Buns and toppings

Equipment

  • A ring mold ¼ inch wider than the bun
  • Waxed paper

Method

1

Divide the meat evenly and roll into balls.

2

On waxed paper, press each into the ring evenly. Lift the ring.

3

Heat the pan until wisps of smoke come off it.

4

Flip a patty onto your palm, peel the paper, and lay the flat side down. It should sizzle loudly.

5

Season the top. Don’t touch it for 4–5 min, until moisture beads on top.

6

Scrape deep underneath and flip. Cook to doneness, season again, add the cheese.

7

Onto the bun and top as you like.

Shake Shack Burger Secrets

Blend, grind, smash, sauce · Pat LaFrieda via Shake Shack

The blend and build

Lighter blend

  • 25% brisket + 75% chuck

Richer blend

  • 20% short rib + 80% chuck

Build

Technique

Chill the meat below freezing, cut it small, and grind it twice. Cold meat is the secret.

4

Buttermilk Fried Chicken

4 servings · marinate 12–24 hours · A Family Feast

Ingredients

Marinade

  • 2 cups buttermilk
  • 1 cup water
  • 3 tbsp kosher salt
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • 2 tsp garlic powder
  • 6 black peppercorns
  • 2 sprigs rosemary, 8 sprigs thyme

Dredge

  • 2 more cups buttermilk
  • 3 cups flour
  • 1½ tsp kosher salt · 1 tsp white pepper
  • ¼ tsp cayenne
  • 1½ tsp each garlic and onion powder
  • 1 tsp paprika

Frying

  • 3 cups or more lard

Meat, Chicken & Fish · Method

Buttermilk Fried Chicken

4 servings · marinate 12–24 hours · A Family Feast

Method

1

Cut the chicken into 10 pieces (breasts halved again) and put in a gallon zip bag.

2

Mix 2 cups buttermilk with the water, salt, sugar, garlic powder, peppercorns and herbs. Marinate the chicken in the fridge 12–24 hours.

3

Discard the marinade, rinse the chicken, and rest it on a rack at room temperature 1 hour.

4

Whisk the dredge dry ingredients in one bowl; put 2 cups buttermilk in another.

5

Lard halfway up a cast iron skillet. Heat to 340°F and never higher. Oven on warm with a rack in a sheet pan.

6

7

  • ¼ cup raisins (optional)

Baking & Sweets · Method

Butter Tarts

Makes 12 · 45 min, plus chilling · Sara Bonisteel, NYT Cooking (2018)

Method

1

Pastry: mix the flour and salt. Rub in the butter or lard until it’s in pea-size pieces.

2

Mix the water, egg yolk and vinegar; stir it into the flour with a fork. Add 1 tbsp more water if it looks dry.

3

Knead a few times, shape into a flat square, wrap and chill at least 30 min.

4

Roll it out on a floured surface to about 16 by 12 inches, ⅛ to ¼ inch thick.

5

Cut 12 circles with a 4-inch cutter (or a clean 28-oz can). Press each into a standard muffin cup, edge flush with the pan. Chill.

6

Cover the raisins with hot tap water to plump. Heat the oven to 425°F.

7

Filling: mix brown sugar and salt, then beat in the butter by hand until smooth. Mix in the vanilla and egg. No electric mixer: it adds too much air.

8

Drain the raisins; put 7 or 8 in each shell.

9

Fill each shell about halfway. Set the tin on a baking sheet. Bake 13–15 min for runny, 17–19 min for firmer.

10

After a few minutes, run a knife around each tart. Cool completely in the tin, then pop them out.

Runnier: use ⅓ cup maple, corn or golden syrup plus ⅔ cup brown sugar.

Bitters: Three to Keep

For the home bar · Art of Manliness

The three

  • Angostura: the classic. Cinnamon and clove. Old Fashioned, Manhattan.
  • Peychaud’s: anise, licorice, vanilla. The Sazerac.
  • One you like: cherry is sweeter, grenadine-like.

Taste test

1

Make three basic Old Fashioneds: ¼ cup (2 oz) bourbon, 2 tbsp (1 oz) simple syrup or a sugar cube, and a couple of dashes of bitters. One bitters per drink.

2

Pour a couple of sips of each into tasting glasses.

3

Compare: Angostura is the classic Old Fashioned; cherry is the most dessert-like; Peychaud’s tastes of licorice.

Also try: herbal bitters in a Dark and Stormy, fruit bitters in a mocktail, nut bitters in a nip of Scotch.
